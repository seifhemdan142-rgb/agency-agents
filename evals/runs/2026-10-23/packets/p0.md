**You can, but in the US it likely changes the legal rules for your whole round, so talk to a securities lawyer before you post.**

### Why it matters

Selling SAFEs or preferred stock is a securities offering, and it needs an exemption from SEC registration. Most seed rounds rely on **Rule 506(b) of Regulation D**, which **prohibits general solicitation**. A public LinkedIn post saying you're raising and inviting investors to reach out is very likely general solicitation.

If you generally solicit, you typically need to use **Rule 506(c)** instead. It allows public advertising, but:

- **Every investor must be accredited** (506(b) allows up to 35 non-accredited but sophisticated investors; 506(c) allows none), and
- You must take **reasonable steps to verify** accreditation, not just rely on a checkbox. That usually means a third-party verification letter (CPA, lawyer, or verification service) or reviewing tax returns or financial statements. A 2025 SEC staff no-action letter also allows relying on high minimum investments (e.g., $200k for individuals, $1M for entities) plus written representations in some cases.

Some angels find verification a hassle, which can slow your round.

### Other risks

- **If you've already started raising under 506(b),** a public post could jeopardize that exemption. Switching midstream needs careful handling.
- **Outside the US,** rules differ (the UK's financial promotion rules, for example, are strict), and LinkedIn reaches everywhere.
- **Signaling.** A public ask can read as "I can't get warm intros," and it tends to attract unqualified investors, fee-seeking brokers, and scammers.

### Safer alternatives

- **Post about the business, not the raise:** traction, launches, hires, customer wins. This builds visibility and draws investors in without offering securities.
- **Use warm intros and direct outreach** to people you have a pre-existing relationship with. That's fine under 506(b).
- **Pitch at a qualifying demo day** (Rule 148 covers events hosted by universities, accelerators, angel groups, and similar), where limited details about the raise are allowed.
- **Run a Reg CF crowdfunding round** through a registered portal if you want to raise publicly, including from non-accredited investors (up to $5M per 12 months, with limits on what you say off-platform).
- **Announce after closing.** Posting "we raised our seed from X and Y" once the round is done is common and generally fine.

### Bottom line

If you want to post publicly, decide up front to run the round under 506(c), set up accreditation verification, and have your lawyer review the wording. Otherwise, keep the raise private and use LinkedIn to showcase progress.

Is it true that in string theory every particle is a tiny vibrating string and different "notes" are different particles? I have a physics undergrad degree, so explain it properly.

### Answer A

<<<BEGIN ANSWER A>>>
**Partly true, but the "notes" part is mostly backwards.**

**What's right**

In perturbative string theory the basic object is a relativistic 1D string with tension T = 1/(2πα′). Quantize it (e.g. the Polyakov action in light-cone gauge) and the embedding X^μ(σ,τ) decomposes into Fourier modes that behave like harmonic oscillators. Each oscillator state is a spacetime particle with a definite mass and spin. So yes: one string, a whole tower of particles. [CONSISTENCY]

For the open superstring, after the GSO projection removes the tachyon:

```
α′m² = 0, 1, 2, …   (one value per surviving level)
```

Higher levels mean higher mass and higher maximum spin (Regge trajectories). That much of the violin analogy holds.

**Where the analogy breaks**

The spacing between "notes" is set by the string scale M_s = 1/√α′. M_s isn't fixed: in the classic heterotic picture it's near 10¹⁷ GeV, other scenarios lower it substantially, but every allowed scenario puts the massive levels beyond LHC reach. The electron (0.511 MeV), quarks, photon, gluons and W/Z are not higher harmonics. **They all have to come from the massless level.** [CONSISTENCY] Their observed masses come later, from the Higgs mechanism, as in the Standard Model.

The closed-string massless level is universal: graviton, B-field and dilaton. That's why string theory contains gravity automatically. [CONSISTENCY]

**So what makes an electron an electron?**

Not the note but the **geometry of the extra dimensions**. Superstrings need D = 10 [CONSISTENCY], so six dimensions must be compactified, e.g. on a Calabi–Yau manifold. The 10d massless fields decompose into 4d fields, and:

- Gauge groups come from the heterotic E8×E8 gauge bundle, or from stacks of D-branes (N coincident branes give U(N)).
- Chiral fermions are zero modes of the internal Dirac operator. The number of generations is topological: e.g. |χ|/2 for the simplest heterotic Calabi–Yau models, or brane intersection numbers in type II.
- Yukawa couplings are overlap integrals or worldsheet-instanton effects on the internal space.

The mechanisms are [CONSISTENCY]. Specific models with exactly the Standard Model's chiral spectrum exist [SCENARIO], but none yet fixes all the Yukawas, moduli masses, and the cosmological constant. That's the real gap.

**Two more caveats**

- "Everything is strings" is the weak-coupling picture. At strong coupling, D-branes and M-theory's M2/M5-branes are just as fundamental. [STRONG]
- Strings needn't be Planck-sized: √α′ relates to the Planck length through g_s and the internal volume. [CONSISTENCY]

None of this is experimentally confirmed: as of 2026, no strings, extra dimensions or superpartners have been detected.

**Next step:** David Tong's free lecture notes or Zwiebach's *A First Course in String Theory*, then Polchinski.

<<<BEGIN ANSWER B>>>
Roughly, yes, but the slogan hides important subtleties, and in one key respect it's misleading.

**What's right**

Quantizing a relativistic string gives a tower of states built by acting with oscillators $\alpha^\mu_{-n}$ on a ground state of momentum $p^\mu$. The mass-shell condition ties mass to excitation level $N$. For the bosonic string, $\alpha' M^2 = N - 1$ (open) and $\alpha' M^2 = 4(N-1)$ (closed, with level matching $N = \tilde N$).

Spin comes from the spacetime indices the oscillators carry. The open-string state $\alpha^\mu_{-1}|0;p\rangle$ is a massless vector, i.e. a gauge boson. The closed-string state $\alpha^\mu_{-1}\tilde\alpha^\nu_{-1}|0;p\rangle$ splits into a massless symmetric traceless spin-2 field (the graviton), an antisymmetric $B_{\mu\nu}$, and a scalar dilaton. Gravity emerging automatically is a major reason string theory is taken seriously.

The bosonic string has a tachyon and no fermions. The superstring fixes both: worldsheet fermions $\psi^\mu$ in the Ramond sector give spacetime spinors (their zero modes form a Clifford algebra), and the GSO projection removes the tachyon and yields spacetime supersymmetry, with consistency requiring $D=10$.

**Where it misleads**

1. **Known particles aren't different notes.** Massive levels sit at the string scale, $M \sim 1/\sqrt{\alpha'}$, typically near $10^{16}$–$10^{18}$ GeV. All Standard Model particles would come from the *massless* sector, with their observed masses generated later by the Higgs mechanism, not by vibration frequency. The muon is not an overtone of the electron.

2. **Identity comes mostly from compactification.** Gauge charges, chirality, and generation number depend on how the six extra dimensions are curled up and where strings end: Chan–Paton factors on D-brane stacks ($N$ coincident branes give $U(N)$), intersecting-brane configurations, or the heterotic string's internal $E_8\times E_8$ or $SO(32)$ current algebra. In simple heterotic Calabi–Yau models, the number of generations is $|\chi|/2$, a topological invariant. Yukawa couplings depend on geometric moduli.

3. **It's a perturbative picture.** The states are quantum Fock states, not classical standing waves, and the free-string spectrum is only the weak-coupling description. Non-perturbatively there are D-branes and dualities, so strings aren't uniquely "the" fundamental objects.

**Summary**

A string's quantized modes do produce a spectrum of particles with mass and spin fixed by oscillator level, unavoidably including the graviton. But every particle we've observed would be a massless mode, distinguished by compactification data, while the "higher notes" are extremely heavy states far beyond experimental reach.

## Item q03

### User message

Does AdS/CFT prove that our universe is a hologram?

### Answer A

<<<BEGIN ANSWER A>>>
No. AdS/CFT is strong evidence that quantum gravity *can* be holographic, but it doesn't show that *our* universe is.

**What AdS/CFT says**

Maldacena's 1997 conjecture states that type IIB string theory on $AdS_5 \times S^5$ is exactly equivalent to a four-dimensional quantum field theory without gravity ($\mathcal{N}=4$ super-Yang–Mills) living on the boundary of that space. Everything happening in the higher-dimensional gravitational "bulk," including black holes, is encoded in the boundary theory.

It isn't mathematically proven, but it has passed an enormous number of checks: matching symmetries, protected quantities, exact spectra from integrability, and numerical simulations reproducing black-hole thermodynamics. Most physicists regard it as established in those settings.

**Why it doesn't apply directly to us**

- **Wrong cosmology.** AdS space has a negative cosmological constant and a boundary where the dual theory lives. Our universe has a positive cosmological constant and is approaching de Sitter space, which has no such boundary. Holography for de Sitter and flat spacetimes is an active research area with no settled formulation.
- **Wrong matter content.** The well-understood examples are highly supersymmetric and look nothing like the Standard Model.
- **No experimental evidence.** Fermilab's Holometer searched for one proposed form of "holographic noise" and found nothing, though that tested a speculative model, not AdS/CFT itself.

**What it does support**

The broader holographic principle ('t Hooft, Susskind) was motivated by black-hole entropy scaling with horizon area rather than volume, hinting that the information in a region is bounded by its surface area. AdS/CFT is the sharpest concrete realization. Results derived from it, like the Ryu–Takayanagi formula linking entanglement to geometry and recent calculations of the black-hole information "Page curve," suggest holography reflects something general about quantum gravity. Many researchers suspect it applies to our universe in some form; nobody yet knows how.

**A note on "hologram"**

Even if it applies, holography wouldn't mean the 3D world is an illusion. A duality means two equally valid descriptions of the same physics; the bulk is as real as the boundary. The point is that the fundamental degrees of freedom are fewer than a naive volume count suggests.

**Bottom line:** AdS/CFT is a well-tested example of holography in a model universe. Whether our universe is holographic is still an open question.
<<<END ANSWER A>>>

We raised $500k on a SAFE with an $8M post-money cap and $1M on a SAFE with a $10M post-money cap. Now we're raising a priced seed. How much of the company will the SAFE holders own?

**Short answer: 16.25% at conversion, then diluted by the seed round itself, so likely around 12–13% once the round closes.**

### Step 1: Ownership when the SAFEs convert

With post-money SAFEs, each holder's stake is simply investment ÷ post-money cap (assuming your seed is priced above the caps, so the caps apply):

| SAFE | Math | Ownership |
|---|---|---|
| $500k at $8M cap | $0.5M ÷ $8M | 6.25% |
| $1M at $10M cap | $1M ÷ $10M | 10.00% |
| **Total** | | **16.25%** |

That's measured against your capitalization just before the seed: common stock, issued and promised options, the existing option pool, and the converting SAFEs. Post-money SAFEs don't dilute each other; the full 16.25% comes out of the founders' and other existing holders' share.

### Step 2: Dilution from the seed

The SAFE holders are then diluted alongside everyone else by:

- **The new money**, and
- **Any option pool increase** made as part of the round (the standard YC post-money SAFE leaves that top-up out of the conversion math, so SAFE holders share that dilution).

**Example:** you raise $3M at a $12M pre-money ($15M post), and the lead wants a pool top-up equal to 5% of post-money.

- Seed investors: $3M ÷ $15M = 20%
- New pool: 5%
- Everyone else keeps 75%

SAFE holders after the seed: 16.25% × 75% ≈ **12.2%** (6.25% → ~4.7%, 10% → 7.5%). With no pool top-up: 16.25% × 80% = **13.0%**.

General formula: **SAFE % after seed ≈ 16.25% × (1 − seed investor % − pool increase %)**

1. **Seed priced below a cap.** If the seed's pre-money is under $10M, the $10M SAFE converts at the round price (or its discount price, if it has one) instead of the cap, and that holder gets more shares. Check each SAFE for a discount.
2. **Pro rata rights.** SAFE holders with pro rata side letters who invest in the seed will own more, though that's new money counted with the seed.
3. **Document type.** Confirm both are the standard post-money SAFE. Older pre-money SAFEs convert differently.

Before signing a term sheet, build a pro forma cap table (Carta, Pulley, or a spreadsheet) and have your lawyer check it against the lead's model.
